# Cluefolio Voice

## The one-line version

We tell true stories the way a good documentary does: calmly, specifically, and with respect for
the people involved.

## Principles

### 1. Specificity is the drama

Weak: "It was a tragedy beyond imagination."
Strong: "The water was twenty feet deep. The dock was twenty feet away."

Numbers, names, dates, and physical details do the emotional work. Adjectives do not.

### 2. Trust the reader

We never say "you won't believe," "what happened next shocked everyone," or "wait for it."
We give the reader the facts in the best order and let them feel it.

### 3. One idea per paragraph

Mobile reading is vertical and impatient. A paragraph is one beat. Two to four sentences.
White space is part of the design.

### 4. Calm authority

No exclamation marks. No all-caps for emphasis. No emoji unless it carries real meaning
(we generally avoid them in captions; a single arrow or map pin is acceptable).

### 5. Respect the dead

Describe scale and consequence without lingering. Do not aestheticize suffering.
Do not use a death toll as a punchline or a hook gimmick.

### 6. Original phrasing

We do not lift sentences, structures, or signature moves from other creators.
If a phrase feels borrowed, rewrite it.

### 7. End with weight

The last line should land. It can be a fact, a turn, a quiet image, or an honest question.
It should never be a call to "follow for more."

## Sentence rhythm

Vary length deliberately. A long, detailed sentence followed by a short one creates emphasis.

> The ship had been built for speed, modified for capacity, and inspected for everything except
> the one thing that would kill it. Stability.

## Openings we use

- A concrete image: "At 7:28 on a July morning, a 265-foot steamship rolled onto its side in the Chicago River."
- A contradiction: "The ship never left the dock. That is the part people forget."
- A number with a physical anchor: "844 people died in twenty feet of water."

## Openings we ban

- "In [year], something happened that would change everything."
- "Imagine you are..."
- "This is the story of..."
- "You've probably never heard of..."
- "What happened next will..."

## Words we avoid

journey, unbelievable, shocking, chilling, haunting, tragic (as a crutch), iconic, legendary,
"little did they know," "forever changed," "in a matter of minutes" (unless precise).

## Words we like

still, tied, dock, hull, ballast, list, roll, manifest, inquiry, verdict, ledger, unclaimed.

## Platform voice differences

| | Instagram | Facebook |
|---|---|---|
| Length | Tighter, ~1,200–1,800 chars | Can run longer, ~1,800–2,600 chars |
| Line breaks | Frequent, generous | Slightly denser, still short paragraphs |
| Hashtags | 4–6 at the end | 3–4, or none |
| First line | Must work above the "more" fold | Must work in the feed preview |
| Tone | Slightly more visual, immediate | Slightly more explanatory, narrative |

Both must tell the same factual story. Neither is a copy of the other.
