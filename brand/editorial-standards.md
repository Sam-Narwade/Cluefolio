# Editorial Standards & Refusals

Binding on every agent. `standards-editor` enforces at Gate 5 and holds veto power.

## 1. Truth

- Every material claim must trace to a source in `03-source-ledger.md`.
- Direct quotations must come from a primary source or a reputable secondary source that quotes it.
  If we cannot verify the exact wording, we paraphrase without quotation marks.
- Numbers must match a cited source. When sources disagree, we say so.
- Causal claims ("X caused Y") require a source that actually asserts causation, not our inference.
- We distinguish **what happened**, **what a court found**, and **what historians argue**.

## 2. Originality

- We do not copy sentences, paragraph structures, or signature phrases from other creators.
- We may use facts from any reputable source; we write them ourselves.
- We do not present a Wikipedia article, lightly edited, as reporting.
- If our angle is common, we must add verified depth that the common version lacks.

## 3. Imagery

- **Authentic first.** Public-domain archives, government collections, museum open-access, or
  properly licensed stock.
- **Verify the image belongs to the story.** A file named "Eastland" is not proof. Check the
  catalog record, date, and creator.
- **Rights:** record the license, the source URL, and the credit line. "Found on Google" is not a license.
- **AI reconstructions** are permitted only when authentic imagery is unavailable or unusable, and
  must be disclosed in the caption and in `post-metadata.json`.
- **Never** present an AI image as a historical photograph. Never add generated detail to a real
  archival image.
- **No generative fill, no AI upscaling that invents texture, no synthetic faces on real people.**

## 4. Dignity

- Disasters involve identifiable families. We describe scale and consequence without lingering on
  corpses, remains, or recognizable suffering.
- We do not use death imagery as a thumbnail.
- We do not name private individuals in a damaging light absent a clear public interest.
- Victims are people, not set dressing. Where we can, we name them accurately and with care.

## 5. Platform policy

- No graphic violence or gore in feed images.
- No harassment, hate, or targeted negativity.
- No medical or legal advice.
- No misleading health, safety, or financial claims.
- Disclose AI-generated imagery where platforms require it, and where viewers could be misled.
- Respect copyright and attribution requirements.

## 6. Engagement ethics

We do not:
- Ask a question we do not answer to farm comments.
- Use rage bait, tribalism, or manufactured controversy.
- Withhold the resolution to inflate watch time.
- Post the same caption to every platform.
- Buy or trade engagement.

We do:
- End with a genuine open question when the record is genuinely open.
- Invite correction and respond to it honestly.
- Credit archives and researchers.

## 7. Corrections

If a published post contains a material error:

1. Correct the caption or image within 24 hours.
2. Add a visible correction note in the caption or first comment.
3. Log it in the post's `14-postmortem.md`.
4. If the error is structural, unpublish and rebuild.

We do not silently edit and pretend it did not happen.

## 8. AI disclosure language

When used, disclose plainly in the caption:

> Some images in this post are AI-generated reconstructions, not historical photographs. They are
> labeled in the caption.

And label the specific image:

> Image 4: AI reconstruction, not a photograph.

Never bury the disclosure in hashtags.
