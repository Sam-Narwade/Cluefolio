## 1. The assignment

One complete, publication-ready image-led post for Instagram and Facebook, aimed at a US audience. No monthly calendar. This is a new post and must establish the studio's standard for the single-photograph format.

## 2. The angle

> The Hindenburg was coming in for a landing when it caught fire and fell from the sky — 36 people died in thirty-four seconds, and the cause remains officially undetermined.

## 3. Why this story, why now

- The central image is an iconic, instantly recognizable photograph: the airship coming in for a landing, then engulfed in flames. The visual stopping power is immediate and overwhelming.
- It is a genuine aviation-failure story: design, lifting gas, weather, and ground handling converged in a matter of seconds. That makes it relevant to modern readers, not just history buffs.
- The Sam Shere photograph (1937) is public domain and abundant, so no AI reconstruction is needed and no disclosure is required.
- It is widely known relative to its scale, but the detailed cause — and the specific role of the photograph — is under-known, giving it "I never knew this" share value.
- The story illustrates the tension between well-publicized danger and the moment of actual catastrophe, which resonates with contemporary audiences.

## 4. Non-negotiable facts

These are verified before writing and may not change without a new fact-check:

- The Hindenburg caught fire on May 6, 1937, while approaching the mooring mast at Naval Air Station Lakehurst, New Jersey.
- 35 of the 97 people on board died (13 passengers, 22 crew), plus 1 ground crew member was killed, for a total of 36 fatalities.
- 62 of the 97 people on board survived — the largest survival rate for a disaster of its kind.
- The airship was filled with hydrogen, not helium, for lift.
- The disaster occurred during an attempted docking/landing in rainy weather conditions.
- The Sam Shere photograph captured the moment of the fire as the airship was coming in for a landing; it is the most published image of the disaster.
- The cause is most likely leaking hydrogen ignited by static electricity, but no single theory has been proven definitively.
- Captain Max Pruss was at the controls during the approach and landing.

## 5. Audience

US adults 25–55 who follow aviation history, true-story accounts, and documentary content on Instagram and Facebook. They save and share posts that teach them something specific they did not know. They dislike being manipulated and they notice errors.

## 6. Platforms and format

- Instagram: 4:5, 1080×1350, caption ≤2,200 characters, 4–6 hashtags. Single primary photograph.
- Facebook: 4:5, 1080×1350, platform-native caption, 3–4 hashtags. Single primary photograph.
- Image count: one primary photograph only. Determined by the Art Director after the outline (see `08-visual-plan.md`).

## 7. Known risks

| Risk | Mitigation | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Death-toll figures differ between sources (35 vs 36) | Use the verified figure of 35 on board + 1 ground crew, total 36; note the distinction in the caption | `fact-checker` |
| Cause of disaster is disputed | Present the most likely theory (leaking hydrogen + static electricity) as the current best explanation; avoid stating it as proven fact | `story-editor` |
| Sensitivity of a mass-casualty story | No corpse or body-recovery imagery; no lingering on suffering | `standards-editor` |
| Presenting speculation as fact | Attribute the cause theory to investigators; never state "the Hindenburg was caused by" as definitive | `fact-checker` |
| Image quality at 1080×1350 | Use the highest-resolution version of the Sam Shere photo; ensure crop holds visual impact | `art-director` |

## 8. Constraints

- No paid APIs, subscriptions, or credits.
- No posting or publishing. Deliver a package for manual publishing.
- Respect archive rate limits and terms.
- Single primary photograph only — no carousel, no slideshow.
