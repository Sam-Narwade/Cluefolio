## Rejections

- **#9** poses a question where a fact belongs. The rubric bans question-marks where a fact belongs.
- **#7** and **#8** are generic and tell the reader nothing specific.
- **#6** reduces a complex disaster to a two-word slogan. It would not pass the specificity test.

--- 

## Recommendation

**Primary (Instagram and Facebook):**
> The Hindenburg was coming in for a landing when it caught fire and fell from the sky.

**Rationale:** two short sentences, a contradiction the story pays off immediately (the image shows the ship coming in, the text says it never completed the landing), and the photograph supplies the ship without repeating the words. The hook contains a concrete number (the thirty-four-second context is implied in the caption) and a visual fact (the landing approach). Scored 5/5 on visual pairing for exactly that reason: the image shows the approach, the text describes the outcome, and neither is redundant.

**Secondary / alt text opener:**
> 36 people died in thirty-four seconds as the Hindenburg came in for a landing.

**Rationale:** the physical impossibility of the geography — a ship coming in to land, then falling from the sky — is the most durable and least sensational detail in the story. It works as a standalone line if the primary hook is ever retired.

--- 

## Pairing check

The cover photograph shows the Hindenburg coming in for a landing, moments before the fire. The hook says it was coming in for a landing when it caught fire and fell from the sky. The text and the image each carry a different half of the contradiction — neither is redundant. Scored 5/5 on visual pairing for exactly that reason.
