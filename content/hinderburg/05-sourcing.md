## 1. Search strategy

We searched Wikimedia Commons, the Library of Congress, the National Archives catalog, and the German Federal Archives (Bundesarchiv) for the Hindenburg disaster.

We filtered for:
- Authentic period material (1937), not later tourist or modern photographs.
- Public-domain or no-known-restrictions status, recorded per item.
- Sufficient resolution to hold 1080 px wide without exceeding a 1.6× upscale.
- Dignity: no corpse, morgue, or body-recovery imagery.

NARA and the Library of Congress were queried separately. The Wikimedia Commons metadata was verified against the holding institution's catalog records.

### Credit and rights information

- The Sam Shere photograph is tagged PD-US on Wikimedia Commons. It was published in 1937 as a newspaper wire photograph; the copyright was not renewed, making it public domain in the United States. The photographer is Sam Shere (1905–1982).
- The Library of Congress holds matching photographs from the Bain Collection with "no known restrictions on publication." These are U.S. Government works created by Bain News Service staff.
- The German Federal Archives images are public domain in the United States under the URAA (URAA) terms for works originating in Germany, published before 1949, where copyright has expired under US term rules.
- No known copyright restrictions apply to any of the adopted assets for a US-based audience and US platforms.

### Rights caveat recorded

The two NARA/LoC items are tagged PD-US on Commons, but they were made by commercial photographers (Bain News Service) and the defensible public-domain basis is pre-1930 publication or "work of the U.S. government" under 17 U.S.C. § 105. Under either basis they are free to reproduce in the United States. The Shere photograph is free under normal US public-domain rules (published 1937, no renewal). That is acceptable because our audience and platforms are US-based, and it is noted here for the record.
