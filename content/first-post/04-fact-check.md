## 2. The material errors found

**Error 1 — a fabricated name.**
The draft named "Paul Steinbrecher" as a defendant whose extradition was refused. No person by that
name is connected to this case in any source consulted. The six men indicted were Arnold, Hull,
Reid, Eckliff, Pedersen, and Erickson. **Removed.**

**Error 2 — wrongly asserting a criminal conviction.**
The draft repeated the widely circulated claim that chief engineer Joseph Erickson was the only
person convicted. This is false. What actually happened:

- A grand jury indicted the owners, the captain, the chief engineer, and federal inspectors.
- The case was moved to Grand Rapids, Michigan.
- In February 1916 the defendants were found **not guilty**, and the court **refused extradition**
  to Illinois, so no criminal trial was ever held in Chicago.
- The "Erickson was guilty" story comes from a **1933 civil ruling** that attributed the disaster to
  his use of the ballast tanks. Erickson had died in 1919. The civil claims ended with liability
  limited to the wreck's salvage value, and the families ultimately received no damages.

**Removed from the draft and the caption.**
