## 1. Audience

US adults 25–55 who follow history, engineering failures, and true-story accounts. They are the
people who save a post to read later and send it to one specific friend. They respond to concrete
detail and they punish exaggeration. The secondary audience is Chicago-area readers, who will
recognize the river and the Loop reference and feel a local stake.

## 2. Search and discovery terms

Words people actually use for this event, and where they appear naturally:

| Term | Where it appears |

| 1915 Chicago | Dateline on the cover, caption |
| Great Lakes shipwreck | Caption, hashtag |

No keyword stuffing. Each term appears once, in a sentence that needs it.

## 3. Hashtags

**Instagram (5):**

| Tag | Reason |
|---|---|
| `#EastlandDisaster` | The exact event name; the highest-intent tag |
| `#ChicagoHistory` | Strong local and topical community |
| `#GreatLakes` | Places the story in a region people follow |
| `#MaritimeHistory` | Reaches the shipwreck and naval-history audience |

**Facebook (4):** the same set minus `#ForgottenHistory`, which performs poorly there.

Rejected: `#history`, `#disaster`, `#1915` — too broad to route to anyone, and `#disaster` attracts
graphic content we do not want the post adjacent to.

## 4. Carousel order rationale

Image 1 must earn the swipe on its own. It shows the ship already on its side, which is visually
arresting at thumbnail size and does not repeat the headline — the text says "never left the dock"
and the image shows the result. Image 2 then answers "what was it?" and the rest deliver the turns.
The closing plate gives the reader something to hold: the river today.

## 5. Share triggers

The lines most likely to be screenshotted or quoted:

1. **"The Eastland was still tied to the dock when it killed 844 people."** — the contradiction,
   shareable on its own as a fact.
2. **"That shallow, narrow gap is why hundreds climbed across the exposed hull and lived."** — the
   counterintuitive mechanism; the part people retell.
3. **"The dock is a short walk from the Loop. The water is 20 feet deep. Most days, nobody looks
   down."** — the ending, which carries the "I've walked past this" feeling.

## 6. Comment prompt

Built into the ending rather than bolted on: readers who know the river will say so, and readers who
did not know the event will say that. We do not ask a question. The honest question — how does a
disaster of this scale become invisible? — is implied by the last line and left open.

## 7. Timing

**Recommendation: Tuesday or Wednesday, 12:00–1:00 p.m. Eastern, or Sunday 7:00–9:00 p.m. Eastern.**

Reasoning: history and long-form accounts perform well in the midday US lunch window and on Sunday
evenings, when mobile reading peaks. Avoid Monday mornings, when feed competition is highest and
dwell time is lowest. This is a reasoned default, not a measured result for this account, which has
no history yet.
