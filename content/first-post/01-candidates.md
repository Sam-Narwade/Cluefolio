# Story Candidates — Gate 1

**Owner:** `editorial-director`
**Date:** 2026-10-19
**Gate:** G1 — Greenlight

---

## Scoring key

Each criterion 1–5. Maximum 40. A candidate must score ≥30 to be greenlit, and must have a
one-sentence angle a stranger understands.

---

## Candidate A — SS Eastland, Chicago, 1915

> A steamship capsized while still tied to its dock, killing 844 people in twenty feet of water.

| Criterion | Score | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Hook strength | 5 | "The ship never left the dock" is a genuine contradiction |
| Evidence quality | 5 | NARA, LoC, congressional record, historical society, inquiry testimony |
| Emotional impact | 5 | Mass casualty, young victims, entire families; handled with restraint |
| Visual potential | 5 | Extensive public-domain photography from July 1915 |
| Narrative depth | 5 | Mechanism, regulation, negligence, legal failure, second life of the ship |
| US relevance | 5 | Chicago; American workers and families |
| Originality | 4 | Well documented but under-known to a general audience; our angle is the dock |
| Share/save potential | 5 | "I never knew this" plus engineering explanation worth saving |
| **Total** | **39/40** | |

**Angle:** The Eastland was a known stability failure that a well-intentioned safety law made
worse, and it killed hundreds of people without ever leaving the wharf.

**Verdict:** SELECTED.

## Candidate B — The Halifax Explosion, 1917

> Two ships collided in a harbor and the resulting blast flattened a city.

- Hook 4, Evidence 5, Emotion 5, Visuals 4, Depth 4, US relevance 3 (Canadian), Originality 3,
  Share/save 4 → **32/40**
- Strong, but heavily covered on social platforms already. Our angle would not be distinctive.
- Visual archive is good but more contested for rights.

**Verdict:** Hold for a future post.

---

## Candidate C — The Triangle Shirtwaist Fire, 1911

> A factory fire killed 146 garment workers and changed American labor law.

- Hook 4, Evidence 5, Emotion 5, Visuals 3, Depth 5, US relevance 5, Originality 2,
  Share/save 4 → **33/40**
- Extremely well covered; 2026 marks 115 years, but the story is a staple of school curricula.
- Originality is the weak point.

**Verdict:** Reject for Post 001. Too familiar to carry our "I never knew this" promise.

---

## Candidate D — The Great Molasses Flood, Boston, 1919

> A tank of molasses burst and sent a wall of syrup through a city street.

- Hook 5, Evidence 4, Emotion 3, Visuals 2, Depth 3, US relevance 5, Originality 3,
  Share/save 5 → **30/40**
- The hook is excellent but the visual archive is thin and the emotional stakes are weaker.
- Risk of reading as trivia rather than history.

**Verdict:** Hold.

---

## Candidate E — The Cocoanut Grove Fire, 1942

> A nightclub fire killed 492 people and produced the first modern burn treatment.

- Hook 4, Evidence 5, Emotion 5, Visuals 3, Depth 4, US relevance 5, Originality 4,
  Share/save 4 → **34/40**
- Strong contender. Loses to Eastland on visual availability and on the uniqueness of the angle.

**Verdict:** Hold for a future post.

---

## Decision

**Greenlit:** Candidate A — SS Eastland.

**Reason:** It scores highest on every axis that matters, it has an irreducible hook in a single
sentence, and it has an abundant, free, authentic visual archive. It also sets the studio standard:
a famous-in-Chicago, under-known-everywhere event told through primary sources and engineering.

**Angle locked as written above.**
