# Narrative Outline — The Ship That Never Left the Dock

**Slug:** `first-post`
**Owner:** `story-editor`
**Gate:** G4 — Story approval
**Date:** 2026-10-19

Structure: hook → context → development → turning point → resolution → ending.
Every beat cites the claim IDs it depends on.

---

## Beat 1 — Hook

**Purpose:** deliver the contradiction in one breath.

> The Eastland was still tied to the dock when it killed 844 people.

Two short sentences. A concrete number and an impossible fact. Nothing withheld that the story
does not immediately pay off.

Claims: C01, C03

---

## Beat 2 — Context

**Purpose:** establish why 2,500 people were on a steamship at 7 a.m. on a Saturday.

Western Electric's employees and their families were bound for a company picnic in Michigan City,
Indiana. The morning was cool. The gangplank was still down. Nobody had gone anywhere.

Claims: C05, C03, C04

---

## Beat 3 — Development (1): the known flaw

**Purpose:** show this was not a freak accident.

The *Eastland* had nearly gone over before — in 1904 with roughly 3,000 aboard, and again in 1912.
Its stability was marginal by design.

Claims: C10, C16

---

## Beat 4 — Development (2): the law that made it worse

**Purpose:** the turn the reader does not expect.

After the *Titanic*, new safety rules required more lifeboats. To keep its passenger license, the
*Eastland* added boats high on its upper decks. The weight went exactly where the ship could least
afford it.

Claims: C11

---

## Beat 5 — Turning point

**Purpose:** the physical event, plainly told.

Shortly after 7 a.m., the ship rolled onto its side. Water came into the lower decks fast.

Claims: C03, C04

---

## Beat 6 — Why the toll was so high, and why so many lived

**Purpose:** the cruel geometry that makes this story unique.

The river was about 20 feet deep and the ship lay about 20 feet from the wharf. That shallow,
narrow space is why hundreds climbed out alive — and why hundreds below deck had minutes, not hours.

Claims: C06, C07

---

## Beat 7 — Resolution

**Purpose:** what happened to the dead, the ship, and the people responsible.

844 dead. 590 of them under 25. Twenty-two entire families gone. Indictments followed; the criminal
cases ended without anyone held responsible. The ship was raised within weeks, sold to the Navy,
renamed USS *Wilmette*, and served into the 1940s.

Claims: C01, C08, C09, C12, C13, C14

---

## Beat 8 — Ending

**Purpose:** land the meaning without instructing the reader.

The dock is a short walk from the Loop. The water is 20 feet deep. Most days, nobody looks down.

No call to action. No "follow for more." The final image is a wide panorama of the wreck, and the
caption simply stops.

Claims: none (reflection grounded in verified C06, C07)
