Briefs

What each post is for

A brief is not a script. It is a reason to spend research time, plus the specific questions that research has to answer.

Post 001 · Disasters / Engineering failure

The Ship That Never Left the Dock

Wednesday, Oct 21, 2026 · provisional score 37/40 · ready

A steamship capsized while still secured to its dock, killing 844 people in twenty feet of water, twenty feet from shore.

Why it earns a slot
The strongest single story in the slate: an impossible fact in one sentence, real engineering substance, and an abundant free visual archive.
Sources to verify
U.S. National Archives (NAID 281840, 281841); Library of Congress, Prints & Photographs Division; H.Res. 375, 114th Congress; Eastland Disaster Historical Society
Imagery outlook
Excellent. Six authentic public-domain frames; renderer complete.
Risks
None outstanding. Two common false claims were removed at Gate 2.

Post 002 · Disasters / Aviation failure

The Airship That Fell From the Sky

Wednesday, Oct 28, 2026 · provisional score 38/40 · ready

The Hindenburg caught fire while landing at Lakehurst, New Jersey. Thirty-six people died in about thirty-four seconds.

Why it earns a slot
An iconic public-domain photograph, a hook that fits in one sentence, and a cause that is still genuinely unresolved.
Sources to verify
Wikimedia Commons, Hindenburg disaster photograph; U.S. National Archives, Hindenburg investigation file; Library of Congress, Bain Collection; Airships.net (context)
Imagery outlook
Excellent. Authentic 1937 photograph of the airship in flames.
Risks
Death toll must distinguish 35 on board from 36 total. Cause must stay qualified.

Post 003 · Disasters / Wildfire

The Fire That Was Forgotten

Wednesday, Nov 4, 2026 · provisional score 36/40 · draft

On the same night Chicago burned, a wildfire in Wisconsin killed an estimated 1,500 to 2,500 people, the deadliest in American history, and almost nobody remembers it.

Why it earns a slot
The Chicago comparison is a built-in hook and a genuine historical injustice. The purest 'I never knew this' story on the slate.
Sources to verify
Wisconsin Historical Society; National Weather Service historical records; Peshtigo Fire Museum; Contemporary Wisconsin and Chicago newspapers, 1871
Imagery outlook
Weak. Surviving photographs are scarce and low-resolution. Consider a second visual pass or a text-forward plate.
Risks
Death toll is an estimate and ranges widely. Must be presented as an estimate, never a precise figure.

Post 004 · Engineering failure

The Dam Nobody Would Fix

Wednesday, Nov 11, 2026 · provisional score 37/40 · draft

A private club's earthen dam failed above Johnstown, Pennsylvania, and killed 2,209 people. The club's members included some of the richest men in America.

Why it earns a slot
Class, negligence, and documented warnings ignored. The clearest accountability story available, and the imagery is strong.
Sources to verify
Johnstown Flood Museum; National Park Service; American Society of Civil Engineers; Contemporary Pennsylvania press, 1889
Imagery outlook
Strong. Widely held period photographs, public domain.
Risks
The club's responsibility is documented but the legal outcome needs precision. Avoid a simple morality play.

Post 005 · Disasters / Safety

The Doors That Opened Inward

Wednesday, Nov 18, 2026 · provisional score 37/40 · draft

A Chicago theater fire killed 602 people in about fifteen minutes. Many of the exits were locked, blocked, or opened inward.

Why it earns a slot
A direct safety-failure story with a physical mechanism a reader can picture, and Chicago continuity with Post 001.
Sources to verify
Chicago History Museum; National Fire Protection Association historical records; Cook County coroner's inquest, 1903; Contemporary Chicago press
Imagery outlook
Strong. Period photographs, postcards, and architectural images.
Risks
Graphic subject. No imagery of victims. Verify each specific exit failure before naming it.

Post 006 · Labor / Disasters

The Worst Mining Disaster in American History

Wednesday, Nov 25, 2026 · provisional score 35/40 · draft

An explosion in a West Virginia mine killed at least 362 men and boys. It led directly to the creation of the federal Bureau of Mines.

Why it earns a slot
Labor history with a concrete policy consequence, and a death toll that is still argued over.
Sources to verify
Mine Safety and Health Administration (MSHA); West Virginia State Archives; U.S. Bureau of Mines records; Contemporary West Virginia press
Imagery outlook
Moderate. Period photographs exist but are low-resolution or graphic. Cover is a disclosed reconstruction.
Risks
Death toll varies from 362 to over 500. Child labor is central and needs care. Dignity review required.

Post 007 · Disasters / Maritime

The Blast That Flattened a City

Wednesday, Dec 2, 2026 · provisional score 37/40 · draft

Two ships collided in Halifax harbour and the munitions cargo detonated, killing about 2,000 people and injuring thousands more.

Why it earns a slot
Enormous scale, a clear chain of events, a strong human-response beat, and a warm US-Canada payoff.
Sources to verify
Nova Scotia Archives; Canadian Museum of History; Royal Society of Canada; Contemporary Canadian and US press
Imagery outlook
Strong. Period photographs are public domain in Canada and the US.
Risks
Non-US story; needs a clear reason for a US audience. Verify the death toll and the Turtle Grove story.

Post 008 · Engineering failure

The Wave That Came From a Tank

Wednesday, Dec 9, 2026 · provisional score 36/40 · draft

A storage tank in Boston's North End burst and sent a wall of molasses through the streets, killing 21 people.

Why it earns a slot
The hook is irresistible and the underlying failure, a tank built too thin and never properly tested, is genuinely instructive.
Sources to verify
Boston Public Library; Boston Globe archives, 1919; National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration; Engineering case studies
Imagery outlook
Strong. Period photographs widely held.
Risks
Tone is the whole risk. It reads as trivia if handled lightly. Verify the claimed wave speed and the toll.

Post 009 · Labor / Science

The Women Who Glowed

Wednesday, Dec 16, 2026 · provisional score 36/40 · draft

Factory workers painted watch dials with radium and were told it was safe. Their bodies became the evidence that changed American labor law.

Why it earns a slot
Human, scientific, and legally consequential. It broadens the season beyond disasters into science and labor.
Sources to verify
Argonne National Laboratory; Occupational Safety and Health Administration; Illinois and New Jersey state archives; Federal court records
Imagery outlook
Moderate. The factory photograph is authentic but soft. Portraits are limited and rights vary.
Risks
Portrait rights need per-item verification. Avoid lurid medical detail. Verify names, dates, and legal outcomes.

Post 010 · Disasters / Medicine

The Fire That Changed Burn Care

Wednesday, Jan 6, 2027 · provisional score 35/40 · draft

A Boston nightclub fire killed 492 people. The treatment of its survivors produced the first modern burn protocol.

Why it earns a slot
Held from the Post 001 shortlist. A double payoff: the disaster and the medical legacy that came out of it.
Sources to verify
Boston Public Library; Massachusetts General Hospital archives; National Fire Protection Association; Contemporary Boston press
Imagery outlook
Weak for rights. The best period photographs are still in copyright. Cover is a disclosed reconstruction.
Risks
Graphic subject; no victim imagery. Verify the toll and the specific medical claims, which are often overstated.

Post 011 · Disasters / Safety

The Tent That Burned in Minutes

Wednesday, Jan 13, 2027 · provisional score 36/40 · draft

A circus tent treated with paraffin and gasoline caught fire during a matinee in Hartford, killing 167 people, most of them children.

Why it earns a slot
Devastating and largely forgotten, with a clear materials-failure mechanism and a direct line to modern flame-retardant rules.
Sources to verify
Connecticut Historical Society; National Fire Protection Association; Hartford Circus Fire memorial records; Contemporary Hartford press
Imagery outlook
Strong. The period photograph is public domain and visually powerful.
Risks
The highest emotional content on the slate, involving children. Dignity review is mandatory. Verify the tent treatment claim.
