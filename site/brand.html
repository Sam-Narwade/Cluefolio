Cluefolio Voice

The one-line version

We tell true stories the way a good documentary does: calmly, specifically, and with respect for the people involved.

Principles

1. Specificity is the drama

Weak: "It was a tragedy beyond imagination." Strong: "The water was twenty feet deep. The dock was twenty feet away."

Numbers, names, dates, and physical details do the emotional work. Adjectives do not.

2. Trust the reader

We never say "you won't believe," "what happened next shocked everyone," or "wait for it." We give the reader the facts in the best order and let them feel it.

3. One idea per paragraph

Mobile reading is vertical and impatient. A paragraph is one beat. Two to four sentences. White space is part of the design.

4. Calm authority

No exclamation marks. No all-caps for emphasis. No emoji unless it carries real meaning (we generally avoid them in captions; a single arrow or map pin is acceptable).

5. Respect the dead

Describe scale and consequence without lingering. Do not aestheticize suffering. Do not use a death toll as a punchline or a hook gimmick.

6. Original phrasing

We do not lift sentences, structures, or signature moves from other creators. If a phrase feels borrowed, rewrite it.

7. End with weight

The last line should land. It can be a fact, a turn, a quiet image, or an honest question. It should never be a call to "follow for more."

Sentence rhythm

Vary length deliberately. A long, detailed sentence followed by a short one creates emphasis.

The ship had been built for speed, modified for capacity, and inspected for everything except the one thing that would kill it. Stability.

Openings we use

  • A concrete image: "At 7:28 on a July morning, a 265-foot steamship rolled onto its side in the Chicago River."
  • A contradiction: "The ship never left the dock. That is the part people forget."
  • A number with a physical anchor: "844 people died in twenty feet of water."

Openings we ban

  • "In [year], something happened that would change everything."
  • "Imagine you are..."
  • "This is the story of..."
  • "You've probably never heard of..."
  • "What happened next will..."

Words we avoid

journey, unbelievable, shocking, chilling, haunting, tragic (as a crutch), iconic, legendary, "little did they know," "forever changed," "in a matter of minutes" (unless precise).

Words we like

still, tied, dock, hull, ballast, list, roll, manifest, inquiry, verdict, ledger, unclaimed.

Cluefolio Visual Identity

The idea

An archival research desk, not a tabloid. Warm paper, dark ink, restrained type, and photographs that are allowed to be photographs.

The grid should be recognizable before anyone reads the wordmark.

Canvas

  • Primary: 1080 × 1350 (4:5 portrait) — Instagram and Facebook feed.
  • Secondary: 1080 × 1080 (1:1) — only if a platform crop demands it.
  • Story/Reel cover: 1080 × 1920 — future use, not part of Post 001.
  • Color profile: sRGB. Format: JPEG, quality 92, progressive.
  • Max file size: 8 MB per image (Instagram feed limit is generous; stay well under).

Palette

Never use pure white #FFFFFF for type on paper. Never use pure black #000000 for text.

Typography

  • Display: Georgia Bold — headlines, numerals.
  • Secondary display: Georgia Italic — pull quotes, datelines.
  • Text: Segoe UI — captions, credits, labels.
  • Labels: Segoe UI Semibold, letter-spaced 0.14em, uppercase, 22–26 px.

Rules:

  • Headline max 2 lines on the cover, max 3 on interior.
  • Never stretch or condense type. Never outline it.
  • Minimum body size on-image: 28 px at 1080 wide.
  • Minimum credit size: 20 px.

  • Top rail: wordmark left, category tag right, both in muted ink.
  • Headline sits in the lower third, on a soft gradient scrim for legibility.
  • Accent rule is 120 px wide, 3 px tall.
  • Never place the headline over a busy area without a scrim.

Interior pages (02–06)

  • Full-bleed photograph, slight warm grade, subtle grain.
  • A 40 px paper-colored margin is not used; images bleed to the edge.
  • A bottom credit strip: 96 px tall, paper background, 90% opacity, muted ink text.
  • Optional short caption line above the credit strip (max 90 characters) in Georgia Italic.
  • Page number bottom-right in Segoe UI, muted ink.

Image treatment

  • Grade: warm highlights, slightly lifted blacks, desaturated by 8–12%. Never crush detail.
  • Grain: subtle, 2–4% opacity. Enough to unify scans of different quality.
  • Sharpening: light unsharp mask after resize. Never over-sharpen archival scans.
  • Cropping: crop for composition, never to distort. Never upscale beyond 1.6× original.
  • No fake colorization. If an image is black and white, it stays black and white.
  • No AI fills, no generative expand, no invented detail.

Consistency across a carousel

  • Same palette, same type, same credit strip position on every image.
  • Image 1 is the only one with the headline lockup.
  • Images 2–6 carry a small category tag in the top-left so a screenshot is still identifiable.

Alt text

Every image gets alt text written for a screen-reader user, not for SEO. Describe what is visible and what it shows, in one to three sentences. Do not begin with "Image of."

Example:

A long passenger steamship lies completely on its side in a narrow city river, its hull exposed above the waterline. Small boats cluster alongside, and a few figures stand on the upturned hull.

Hook Rubric

The hook is the first line of the caption and the first frame of the carousel. It has one job: make a specific promise the story keeps.

The three-second test

A scrolling user sees roughly 125 characters and one image. In three seconds they decide.

Ask:

  1. Is there a concrete noun or number in the first six words?
  2. Is there a contradiction, a stake, or an unanswered question?
  3. Does it avoid every banned opening in voice.md?
  4. Does the image add a second, non-redundant piece of information?

Hook patterns that work for us

The impossible fact

The ship never left the dock.

The number with a physical anchor

844 people died in twenty feet of water, twenty feet from shore.

The contradiction

A brand-new safety law helped kill 844 people.

The quiet image

At 7:28 on a July morning, a 265-foot steamship rolled onto its side in downtown Chicago.

The delayed consequence

The captain survived. He was one of the last people to walk off his own ship.

What we never do

  • Withhold the death toll to force a click.
  • Use "you won't believe" or "wait for it."
  • Promise a mystery the story resolves in the first paragraph.
  • Put a question mark where a fact belongs.
  • Use a corpse or a grieving face as the thumbnail.

Worked example

Weak: "This forgotten disaster shocked Chicago. 🧵"

Why: vague, no specifics, engagement bait, emoji as decoration.

Better: "844 people died on a ship that never left the dock."

Why: number, contradiction, honest.

Best: "The ship never left the dock. 844 people died anyway."

Why: two short sentences, a real contradiction, and the image supplies the ship.

Editorial Standards & Refusals

Binding on every agent. standards-editor enforces at Gate 5 and holds veto power.

1. Truth

  • Every material claim must trace to a source in 03-source-ledger.md.
  • Direct quotations must come from a primary source or a reputable secondary source that quotes it.

If we cannot verify the exact wording, we paraphrase without quotation marks.

  • Numbers must match a cited source. When sources disagree, we say so.
  • Causal claims ("X caused Y") require a source that actually asserts causation, not our inference.
  • We distinguish what happened, what a court found, and what historians argue.

2. Originality

  • We do not copy sentences, paragraph structures, or signature phrases from other creators.
  • We may use facts from any reputable source; we write them ourselves.
  • We do not present a Wikipedia article, lightly edited, as reporting.
  • If our angle is common, we must add verified depth that the common version lacks.

3. Imagery

  • Authentic first. Public-domain archives, government collections, museum open-access, or

properly licensed stock.

  • Verify the image belongs to the story. A file named "Eastland" is not proof. Check the

catalog record, date, and creator.

  • Rights: record the license, the source URL, and the credit line. "Found on Google" is not a license.
  • AI reconstructions are permitted only when authentic imagery is unavailable or unusable, and

must be disclosed in the caption and in post-metadata.json.

  • Never present an AI image as a historical photograph. Never add generated detail to a real

archival image.

  • No generative fill, no AI upscaling that invents texture, no synthetic faces on real people.

4. Dignity

  • Disasters involve identifiable families. We describe scale and consequence without lingering on

corpses, remains, or recognizable suffering.

  • We do not use death imagery as a thumbnail.
  • We do not name private individuals in a damaging light absent a clear public interest.
  • Victims are people, not set dressing. Where we can, we name them accurately and with care.

5. Platform policy

  • No graphic violence or gore in feed images.
  • No harassment, hate, or targeted negativity.
  • No medical or legal advice.
  • No misleading health, safety, or financial claims.
  • Disclose AI-generated imagery where platforms require it, and where viewers could be misled.
  • Respect copyright and attribution requirements.

6. Engagement ethics

We do not:

  • Ask a question we do not answer to farm comments.
  • Use rage bait, tribalism, or manufactured controversy.
  • Withhold the resolution to inflate watch time.
  • Post the same caption to every platform.
  • Buy or trade engagement.

We do:

  • End with a genuine open question when the record is genuinely open.
  • Invite correction and respond to it honestly.
  • Credit archives and researchers.

7. Corrections

If a published post contains a material error:

  1. Correct the caption or image within 24 hours.
  2. Add a visible correction note in the caption or first comment.
  3. Log it in the post's 14-postmortem.md.
  4. If the error is structural, unpublish and rebuild.

We do not silently edit and pretend it did not happen.

8. AI disclosure language

When used, disclose plainly in the caption:

Some images in this post are AI-generated reconstructions, not historical photographs. They are labeled in the caption.

And label the specific image:

Image 4: AI reconstruction, not a photograph.

Never bury the disclosure in hashtags.
